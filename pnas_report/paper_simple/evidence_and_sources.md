### Proposed paper wording and placement

**Results.** We used the signing date to give each player’s coverage a before and an after. We used RoBERTa to score the selected Guardian sentences and averaged the scores on each side of the move. We explored whether the post-signing gap persisted after accounting for differences in the players and the articles that covered them. Prior article counts captured how much Guardian attention each player had already received. Recorded market value let us compare signings with similar valuations, and a separate baseline for each transfer season allowed average tone to vary over time.

### What the permutation null means

In [`_run_test`](../notebooks/age_shuffle_outsideprem_toprem.py), the transfer records, origin clubs, recorded seasons, destinations and PL outcomes remain fixed. Binary 30+/under-30 labels are shuffled only among transfers from the same origin club in the same season. Every stratum retains its observed age counts, so the global age denominators also remain fixed. Single-age strata remain unchanged but contribute to the pooled statistic. Main data contain 4,563 strata, including 572 with both age labels; the fixed-player subset contains 2,461, including 195 with both labels. Mixed ages alone do not guarantee that a stratum changes the statistic: its joining outcomes must also vary.

The null assumes that age labels are exchangeable among transfer records within each origin-club/season stratum with respect to the fixed joining outcomes. It tests whether the observed pooled older-minus-younger gap is unusually negative under that conditional rearrangement. It does not randomly assign actual ages, rewire transfers, control every player characteristic, or estimate a causal age effect.

The expected pooled gap need not be zero. Each age group keeps its own distribution across strata, and strata can have different overall joining rates. For a stratum with `n_s` transfers, `a_s` older labels and `j_s` PL arrivals, the expected number of older arrivals under the shuffle is `a_s × j_s / n_s`. Summing those expectations and dividing by the fixed group denominators need not give equal group rates. Thus composition differences remain in the null.

The saved mean of the 999 shuffled gaps is −1.314527914 points for the main analysis and +0.458181539 points for the sensitivity. These are Monte Carlo means, not exact enumerations of the null expectation. The observed gaps lie about 1.11 and 2.40 points below those respective means. Those differences describe position relative to the null; they are not regression-adjusted age effects or a causal decomposition of the raw gaps. Do not describe the full −2.42-point gap as an age disadvantage remaining after adjustment.

### One-sided p-values and evidence strength

The alternative is a lower joining rate for older transfers, so [`_lower_tail_p_value`](../notebooks/age_shuffle_outsideprem_toprem.py) counts shuffled gaps at or below the observed gap, including ties, and returns `(1 + tail count) / (1 + 999)`. Main: 97 tail draws, including 15 ties, give 98/1,000 = 0.098. Sensitivity: 73 tail draws, including 27 ties, give 74/1,000 = 0.074. Seed 2026 supplies separate child streams for the main shuffles, record selection and sensitivity shuffles.

These p-values measure lower-tail extremeness under the specified conditional null and exchangeability assumption. They are not probabilities that H1 or the null is true. Neither falls below 0.05. Agreed interpretation: the descriptive gaps point in H1's predicted direction, with limited evidence against the specified null. Neither a confirmed age-related entry disadvantage nor equal entry rates is established. Do not infer that origin-club/season composition fully explains the gap from failure to reject, or adopt the earlier draft's binary choice between “H1 supported” and “no age gap.”

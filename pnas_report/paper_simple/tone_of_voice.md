# Tone of voice

Working writing guide based on the user's stated preferences. Revise it together as the question and storyline develop.

## Voice and clarity

- Use simple, academic English, direct sentences, and precise verbs.
- Define necessary technical terms when first introduced.
- Give each paragraph one purpose and connect it to the research question or argument.
- Describe findings with language proportionate to their evidence and uncertainty.
- Preserve the distinction between the original questions and findings that emerged during exploration.

## Numerical detail

- Select numbers that establish a finding's size, comparison, uncertainty, or validation.
- Give each reported number its unit and relevant denominator or sample.
- Round consistently to a precision appropriate for interpretation.
- Use tables and captions for supporting numerical detail, keeping the prose focused on the argument.

## Methods and citations

- Explain what a method measures, why it fits the question, and how it was applied here.
- Cite verified original contributions and the relevant implementation or adaptation.
- Refer readers to those sources for technical derivations.
- Retain the study-specific choices needed for reproduction, including data selection, preprocessing, controls, parameters, and validation.
- Attribute claims and theory to sources that directly support them.

## Interpretation

- Describe observational results through associations and comparisons.
- State the limitations of sentiment scores, sample selection, and null models where they affect interpretation.
- Explain how the evidence bears on the question, including findings with substantial uncertainty.
- Keep terminology consistent across the question, figures, results, and discussion.

## Citation conventions

Follow the supplied PNAS template and its citation guidance. Manage references through the template's bibliography system and check that citations support the associated claims.

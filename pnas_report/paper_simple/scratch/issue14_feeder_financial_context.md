# Issue 14 feeder routes and transfer-fee context

## Findings and primary sources

- **Why negotiated fees matter.** For a club that records player registrations as assets, the gain on selling a registration is generally the net disposal proceeds minus its remaining book value. A higher fee on an otherwise identical deal can therefore raise the seller's reported gain before regulatory adjustments. The buying club can record the registration as an asset and charge the cost over the contract, with a maximum of five years under the 2024 licensing rules. This is a possible club-level accounting incentive. [UEFA Club Licensing and Financial Sustainability Regulations, 2024 ed., Annex G.3.4(c) and Annex J.2.1(g) (Faroese Football Association archive)](https://www.fsf.fo/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/20240601_Regulations_CLFS_2024_EN_22-10-2024.pdf)
- **The fee and the amount recognised for UEFA purposes can differ.** For a permanent move between related clubs, the 2024 rules require the selling club to calculate disposal proceeds using the lower of the actual proceeds and the registration's net book value and adjust the restated figures where required. These accounting provisions constrain the gain recognised for regulatory purposes. The 2024 rules also have separate treatment for loans and player exchanges. [UEFA 2024 regulations, Annex G.3.5, G.3.7 and G.4.7](https://www.fsf.fo/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/20240601_Regulations_CLFS_2024_EN_22-10-2024.pdf)
- **Premier League fair-market-value review.** The league's September 2025 summary records that associated-party rules were introduced in December 2021 and amended in March and November 2024. The league says accurate reporting of costs and revenues is important to its Profitability and Sustainability Rules and describes fair-market-value protocols covering permanent and temporary player registrations. [Premier League's 5 September 2025 summary](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4144827/summary-of-associated-party-transaction-and-fair-market-value-rules) · [Premier League 2023/24 handbook, E.65.3.3](https://resources.premierleague.com/premierleague/document/2024/01/04/21175cfd-faa3-4b01-bbad-65894fead686/PL_Handbook_2023-24_DIGITAL_04.01.24-final-.pdf)
- **Direct connection to the busiest route.** In July 2024, UEFA admitted Girona and Manchester City to the 2024/25 Champions League after governance changes to address multi-club ownership. As an additional safeguard for independence, the clubs committed to suspending direct and indirect transfers, including loans, from July 2024 until September 2025, with an exception for agreements entered into before the proceedings opened. The decision concerns eligibility and club independence. [UEFA's 5 July 2024 multi-club ownership decision](https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/028f-1b4ba6fcea09-078845f25cbf-1000--/)
- **Optional finance enforcement context.** UEFA's July 2025 release reports that its 2024/25 football-earnings review scrutinised player swaps, related-party transfers and asset sales and required financial adjustments. The Chelsea settlement summary lists all three transaction categories without allocating the findings to individual player deals. [UEFA's 4 July 2025 financial sustainability review](https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/029b-1e280e8c0d89-0a2f9801ea14-1000--cfcb-first-chambe/) · [Chelsea settlement summary](https://editorial.uefa.com/resources/029b-1e280acc9f0c-a8b2ed192749-1000/chelsea_summary_version_4-year_sa_20250704173903.pdf)

# Public data for Ukrainian drone strikes: source audit

Research note, 27 October 2026. **Scope:** Ukrainian-launched aerial drones aimed at targets in Russia or Russian-occupied Ukraine. This excludes Russian drones attacking Ukraine and Ukrainian front-line FPV missions unless a source explicitly includes them. “Success” can mean four different things: drone not intercepted, arrival near target, verified physical impact, or functional damage. They must not be collapsed into one binary outcome.

## Finding

I found **no public, independently verified register of every Ukrainian drone launched, where each one was intercepted, its intended target, and whether it hit that target**. The closest public sources have different observational units: claimed intercepted drones by Russian *region and night*, reported strikes by *target and date*, or geolocated damage. A Russian interception report cannot establish the total Ukrainian launch denominator; a confirmed hit cannot establish how many drones were sent or how many were lost en route. The observable study is a **reported attack and impact network**, not a per-drone survival or interception-rate network.

## Other useful leads and prior art

- The author-deposited [Zenodo replication package for a 2026 deep-strike geography study](https://zenodo.org/records/22377030) reports 907 Ukraine-to-Russia geolocated event points for January–August 2026 and includes processed data, code and a dictionary. Check its licensing (the record is marked copyright), source provenance, and whether it has drone-specific and outcome fields before reuse. It shows that a basic “strikes spread deeper” descriptive paper already has close prior art.
- [Plichta, *RUSI Journal*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/03071847.2025.2527923), studies Ukraine's one-way attack-drone campaign from mid-2022 to early 2025. The paper investigates the effectiveness of these inexpensive systems; a new project needs a clearer graph-specific question and should cite it.
- [Oryx’s aircraft-loss list](https://www.oryxspioenkop.com/2022/03/list-of-aircraft-losses-during-2022.html) is **not** a suitable interception register: it excludes loitering munitions and reconnaissance drones and avoids shoot-down videos to prevent duplicate listings.
- [VIINA 2.0](https://github.com/zhukovyuri/VIINA/blob/main/README.md) offers open machine-classified news event records with `a_ukr_init`, `t_uav`, and `t_aad` flags plus location and source URL. It is a possible robustness source but a report classifier, not adjudicated missions or outcomes; validate precision and geographic scope before using it as the primary register.

## Feasible research design

The simplest defensible graph is **target-site ↔ target-class** or **Russian region ↔ night**, with weights as *reported strikes or reported impacts* and an evidence/confidence label. Another useful network is **night ↔ region**, with two distinct edge types: Russian claimed interceptions and independently reported impacts. Compare geographic concentration or temporal mixing, but do **not** interpret impact/interception ratios as drone penetration rates: the datasets have incompatible units, incomplete ascertainment, and no launch denominator. Avoid inferred launch-location→interception-location edges, because launch sites and paths are generally not published; the Observatory explicitly substitutes nearest-border origins in its display.

For a course paper, a manageable pilot is a fixed time window of 2026 deep strikes: export Militarnyi's CSV, separately extract the Observatory's Russian regional claims and source-linked impact records, select one target class (e.g. oil facilities), and hand-audit 30–50 matched target-days against source links and satellite evidence. Pre-register outcome classes: `claim only`, `visual/independent impact evidence`, `confirmed physical damage`, `unknown`; code an interception only when explicitly reported. Report coverage and disagreement rather than converting missing outcomes into failures. A valid claim is about **the geography and evidence quality of reported strikes**, not Ukraine's true launch-to-hit success probability.

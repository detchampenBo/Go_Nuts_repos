# Graph models for studying the war in Ukraine

Research note, 27 October 2026. This is a feasibility assessment, not an empirical analysis. A graph method is useful only after specifying what an edge represents, what the observation process misses, and what result would falsify the proposed explanation.

## Assumptions and decision

Assume the course paper can use publicly accessible data and aims to make a modest, reproducible claim about wartime geography or organizational activity. A region-to-region casualty graph is feasible as a **network of documented deaths**, but it cannot by itself measure deployment, recruitment, or military strategy. For a social-network interpretation, the humanitarian organization-to-location network is conceptually stronger. For a spatial conflict-dynamics paper, UCDP events are the safest openly downloadable starting point. These are separate questions and should not be conflated.

| Candidate | Nodes and observed edges | Defensible question | Main feasibility limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Documented military deaths | Origin and death-location oblasts; one directed weighted edge per observed origin–destination pair, optionally by month | Are documented deaths more geographically concentrated or locally assortative than a baseline preserving each oblast's observed origin and destination totals? | Public obituaries are highly selective; flow is not deployment. |
| Conflict events | Oblasts (or grid cells); connect places whose event-count series have statistically supported lagged association, or use a spatial adjacency graph with events as node attributes | Did the geography of reported lethal violence become more synchronized across periods than expected from common trend and geography? | Functional edges are statistical associations, not troop routes or causal influence. |
| Humanitarian presence | Bipartite organization–district edges, optionally split by sector | Is reported partner coverage unusually clustered or redundant relative to organizational size and location demand? | Presence does not quantify services, money, or effectiveness. |
| Internal displacement | Origin and destination oblasts, weighted by displaced persons | Which destinations receive unusually high flows after accounting for origin displacement and destination size? | A complete public origin–destination table was not verified. |
| Cross-border power | European bidding zones/countries; directed physical electricity flows by time interval | How did Ukraine's observed cross-border exchange mix change after grid synchronization? | Small Ukraine-centered subgraph; energy flows depend on capacity and market rules, so attacks alone cannot explain them. |

## 1. Documented-death origin → death-place graph

[UA Losses](https://ualosses.org/en/soldiers/) has individual records with a home location, a location described as “died in the area of,” a death date, military unit when known, and linked public sources; [an individual record](https://ualosses.org/en/soldier/abalmasov-vladyslav-vasylovych-2000-05-24-22-bereka-14th-brigade-of-operational-assignment-soldier/) shows the field structure. Its [methodology page](https://ualosses.org/en/about/) says records come from public announcements, media, family posts, and monuments, and explicitly warns that online coverage is incomplete and uneven—especially for large cities, Transcarpathia, and much of the east and south. The current listing interface contains status categories beyond confirmed deaths, while the About text says missing soldiers are excluded; this apparent inconsistency means the exported data's **status field and inclusion rule must be audited** before any count.

An existing [third-party CSV snapshot](https://github.com/CodeRyan-Zhihu/UALossesDataset/blob/main/soldiers_data_source.csv) and its [analysis script](https://github.com/CodeRyan-Zhihu/UALossesDataset/blob/main/map_analysis.py) expose home and death-area columns, but the CSV is a historical snapshot and is not the source's validated current export. Record-level reconciliation, source citation, extraction date, unknown locations, place-name normalization, and whether “from” is birth, residence, or registration are minimum audit items. Preserve unknown destinations as missing observations rather than assigning them to the home oblast. Ukraine's standard first-level administrative geography is roughly two dozen units, depending on treatment of Kyiv City, Crimea, and Sevastopol; “about 50 regions” likely mixes administrative levels.

The simplest paper is **not** a centrality ranking. Construct an oblast × oblast count matrix for confirmed deaths, then compare observed concentration/assortativity with a null matrix that preserves observed origin and death-place totals (via permutation or fixed-marginal randomization). Repeat by broad time period and with uncertain geocodes excluded. This tests a narrow claim about the *documented* distribution. A high edge weight can arise from an intense battle, a large oblast population, a large mobilized cohort, unit assignments, and obituary/reporting differences; the roster lacks the denominators needed to identify allocation or risk. Population adjustment can answer a different descriptive question, but civilian population is not the at-risk deployed force.

Novelty must come from a falsifiable comparison or measurement audit. [UA/200](https://ua200.observer/) already displays origin, death-place, and time patterns from an open-source roster, so reproducing a choropleth or origin–destination map alone is unlikely to support a paper.

## 2. Conflict-event spatial and functional graphs

The [UCDP download center](https://ucdp.uu.se/downloads/) provides its Georeferenced Event Dataset (GED) as free CSV/Excel with a codebook and API. The current version 26.1 covers lethal organized-violence events at location and day resolution through 2025; monthly [candidate releases](https://ucdp.uu.se/downloads/) extend later with provisional coding. Its [API documentation](https://ucdp.uu.se/apidocs/) permits a reproducible Ukraine extraction. These are **events** and estimated fatalities, not a roster of all soldiers killed. Record the dataset version and coding definition, because revisions can change results.

[ACLED's Ukraine Conflict Monitor](https://acleddata.com/monitor/ukraine-conflict-monitor) advertises a Ukraine/Black Sea event file from 2020 and a file of attacks tagged by civilian-infrastructure class. Its [codebook](https://acleddata.com/methodology/acled-codebook) defines date, actor, event type, subnational location, coordinates and precision, source, and estimated fatalities. However, the current [myACLED access FAQ](https://acleddata.com/myacled-faqs) says Open access is aggregate-only, while Research access has lagged event-level data after registration; the curated download could not be retrieved without the needed access. Treat ACLED event-level analysis as conditional on university-account eligibility. The [Ukraine methodology](https://acleddata.com/methodology/acleds-methodology-ukraine) cautions that military deaths are particularly difficult to estimate, and the [infrastructure-tag methodology](https://acleddata.com/methodology/acleds-methodology-ukraine-civilian-infrastructure-automated-tags) describes automated tagging and review. An oblast × target-class bipartite graph would describe **reported target types**, not physical infrastructure links.

Two honest graph choices:

1. **Geographic graph:** adjacent oblasts are linked by shared border, and event counts/intensity are node attributes. Measure spatial clustering and change points; compare against shuffled time windows or geography-aware baselines. This is a map-based spatial analysis rather than a claim that events created edges.
2. **Functional graph:** nodes are oblasts and directed edges mean one oblast's past event series improves prediction of another's future series beyond each series' own history. Control for autocorrelation, common national shocks, multiple tests, reporting changes, and spatial adjacency. Call this predictive dependence, never military causation.

The second design has direct prior art: [Zanin and Martínez, *Chaos* (2022), DOI 10.1063/5.0091628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07403) built a directed functional network of Ukrainian oblasts from ACLED events in 2021–early 2022 using Granger-type tests and statistical correction. A course paper should either extend the period/question with a clear baseline or choose another graph; it should not present oblast functional networks as a new idea.

## 3. Humanitarian organization–place network

[OCHA defines 3W operational presence](https://knowledge.base.unocha.org/wiki/spaces/imtoolbox/pages/214499412) as “who does what where.” The first-party [HDX HAPI operational-presence documentation](https://hdx-hapi.readthedocs.io/en/latest/data_usage_guides/coordination_and_context/) specifies organization, organization type, sector, reference period, and admin1/admin2 location fields. HAPI standardizes records to organization–sector–admin2 presence and serves the **latest** source resource, not a historical series; updates are irregular. Its [getting-started guide](https://hdx-hapi.readthedocs.io/en/latest/getting-started/) describes CSV/JSON API access and app identifier. The corresponding Ukraine rows still need to be fetched and inspected to establish practical completeness and time coverage.

This yields a real affiliation network: organizations and districts are two node sets, with an edge when an organization reports activity in a district; sector may be an edge label or third mode. Candidate methods include bipartite degree distributions, concentration of partner portfolios, overlap of organizations across districts, and a degree-preserving null model for whether coverage is more clustered than expected. Comparing district degree with independently sourced need estimates could identify *reported presence gaps*, but presence is not service volume, beneficiary coverage, quality, or unmet need. Any inference must condition on differences in who reports to the 3W system. Public [ActivityInfo's Ukraine description](https://www.activityinfo.org/blog/posts/2023-06-13-supporting-response-in-ukraine-and-region.html) confirms that some detailed 5W views are access-restricted, so the standardized public 3W data are the prudent starting point.

## 4. Displacement networks: promising but data gate remains

[IOM's Ukraine Displacement Tracking Matrix](https://dtm.iom.int/ukraine) uses survey and key-informant methods. Its [August 2024 Round 17 IDP report](https://dtm.iom.int/sites/g/files/tmzbdl1461/files/reports/IOM_GPS_R17_IDP_August%202024.pdf) publishes origin and current-location information, some leading origin→destination flows, and within-oblast displacement. This report is **not** a complete public oblast × oblast OD matrix; published marginal totals cannot reconstruct pairwise edges. An earlier [IOM survey report](https://dtm.iom.int/sites/g/files/tmzbdl1461/files/reports/IOM_Gen%20Pop%20Report_R9_IDP_FINAL_0.pdf) warns that oblast estimates are indicative because its sample was designed for macro-regional representativeness. IOM's [registered-IDP dataset page](https://dtm.iom.int/datasets/ukraine-registered-idps-feb-2022-present) says a more detailed version requires access request; its public table does not establish a full OD matrix.

There is a possible alternative survey lead on [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/19064207), but availability of both origin and destination fields in its open microdata was **not verified**. Before selecting this topic, obtain a record-level OD table or approved microdata with sampling weights and assess whether transitions can be estimated reliably at the chosen geography. With such data, use a directed weighted flow graph, compare edge weights against origin/destination-size baselines (e.g. a gravity or configuration model), and report uncertainty. Without it, a valid paper could study *marginal regional displacement*, but not a network of observed moves.

[UNHCR's Refugee Data Portal API](https://refugeedata.unhcr.org/en/documentation/technical-reference/api/) offers public time series by origin and host country. A Ukraine→host-country graph based on refugee stocks is mostly a star; stocks are not border crossings or individual trajectories, and centrality adds little. It could provide context, rather than the main network.

## 5. A physical network option: electricity exchange

[ENTSO-E reports that Ukraine and Moldova synchronized with the Continental European electricity grid on 16 March 2022](https://www.entsoe.eu/news/2022/03/16/continental-europe-successful-synchronisation-with-ukraine-and-moldova-power-systems/); [commercial exchanges began in June 2022](https://www.entsoe.eu/news/2022/06/28/commercial-exchanges-of-electricity-with-ukraine-moldova-to-start-on-30-june/). Its [Transparency Platform data definitions](https://eepublicdownloads.entsoe.eu/clean-documents/Transparency/MoP_Ref2_DDD_v3r4.pdf) include measured physical flows between neighboring bidding zones by market time unit; [API instructions](https://transparency.entsoe.eu/content/static_content/download?path=%2FStatic+content%2Fweb+api%2FIG-for-TP-data-extraction-process.pdf) specify the flow query. This graph has genuinely physical directed edges, weighted by interval energy, and could examine concentration of Ukraine's cross-border exchanges. API registration/token and data availability for all relevant border pairs must be checked. Changes in flow reflect market rules, border capacity and demand as well as attacks, so event attribution requires more than temporal coincidence.

## Recommended paper paths and verification gates

1. **Closest to the original idea:** “Are the origin–death-place connections in publicly documented Ukrainian military deaths more concentrated than expected from the observed origin and death-location totals?” First verify confirmed-death filters, usable two-ended geocodes, date completeness, duplicate handling, and extraction terms. Report results strictly as patterns in the public roster and test sensitivity to the source's stated geographic reporting bias.
2. **Strongest open conflict-event path:** “How did the spatial synchronization of recorded lethal violence change across phases of the war?” First download a versioned UCDP GED extract; define a time window and a null that preserves temporal autocorrelation/common shocks. Explicitly engage the 2022 Zanin–Martínez precedent.
3. **Best social-network semantics:** “Does reported humanitarian partner presence cluster across districts beyond what organizational size and district demand predict?” First inspect actual Ukraine HAPI rows, reference dates, organization-name consistency, and reporting coverage. Analyze bipartite structure, not projected edges alone.

The decisive feasibility checks are data access, enough nonmissing edge-level observations, a reproducible geographic crosswalk, and a null comparison tied to one narrow claim. No source reviewed here by itself identifies Ukrainian troop allocation, operational strategy, or the full distribution of wartime deaths.

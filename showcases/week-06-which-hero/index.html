    HOW THE MATCHING WORKS 1. Every page is a list of numbers. Each word on a hero’s page gets a score: how often it appears there (TF) times how rare it is across all 303 pages (IDF). “the” gets almost nothing; “adamantium” gets a lot, on the pages that use it. tfidf = (1 + log count) × log(303 / pages with the word) 2. So are you. Each answer is one word, weighted by how rare it is. Then we measure the angle between your list and every page’s list: cosine similarity. It cares which words a page leans on, not how long the page is. cos(you, page) = you · page / (|you| |page|) 3. What it gets wrong. Names are rare, so TF-IDF loves them: two pages can look alike just because two heroes share a surname. One rare answer can carry a whole match, and short pages can win on a single word. And you are compared with Wikipedia’s summaries, not the comics.

    What is real, and how are the scores calculated?

    This fictional case uses a deliberately constructed contact network, not Marvel canon or Wikipedia data. All nine people and fourteen mutual contacts are visible. Connections are undirected and each costs one step. Four people are suspects; everyone contributes to the network measurements. Portraits and node sizes identify characters, not scores.

    Degree is the number of direct contacts. Closeness is 8 divided by the sum of shortest distances to the eight other people. BFS reveals those distances one wave at a time. The second clue uses mean distance, the inverse of closeness. The graph is connected, so no distance is infinite.

    Betweenness counts every unordered pair of other people. Shortest routes that tie split one unit of credit equally, and endpoints earn zero. There are 36 pairs across the network; for any one person, eight involve that person as an endpoint, leaving 28 eligible pairs. Dividing their accumulated credit by 28 gives normalized betweenness. Playback shows calculated routes, not real messages; the extra halo area beyond the base ring represents accumulated credit, and partial values are labelled “so far”.

    Every clue rules out one different suspect. A correct deduction clears them for the rest of the case. Measurements come from the displayed graph, and state resets when this page reloads. Centrality describes network position; it cannot establish criminal intent.

    Visual direction: a light table with registered transparent evidence sheets. Each filed sheet annotates the same fixed network; lifting a sheet changes the view, not the recorded deduction.

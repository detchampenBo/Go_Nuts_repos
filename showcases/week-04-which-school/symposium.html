The Symposium

Twenty-four philosophers. One algorithm deciding who sits where.

Aperitif

The guest list

Twenty-four philosophers, from Socrates to Nietzsche, at one long table.

Two guests share a conversation when one of their Wikipedia articles links to the other’s. 110 of the 276 possible pairs do. That is the whole party: 24 nodes, 110 links.

Some guests are more sociable than others. The number of conversations a guest has is their degree.

Tap a guest to see who they talk to.

    A conversation is a link. How many a guest has is their degree.

    First course

    Your seating plan

    Four tables. Seat the room so that guests talk to their tablemates.

    Choose a guest on the table plan, then send them to table A, B, C or D. The meter scores your plan.

    Harmony0.000
    Conversations kept at a table100%
    Expected, for guests this sociable100%

    Why the second term? Two chatty guests would find each other at almost any table, so seating them together earns less. Everyone at one table keeps every conversation, and scores exactly zero.

    Modularity: the share of links inside groups, minus the share you would expect from degrees alone.

    Second course

    The maître d’

    Louvain seats the room by the same score, one guest at a time.

    Every guest starts at a table for one. In a random order, each moves to whichever neighbouring table raises the harmony most. When nobody wants to move, every table becomes a single guest and the process repeats.

    Fifty evenings, fifty random orders. Squares with the same letter are the same seating plan.

    The order is random, so the plan can change. Compare two plans with NMI: 1 is identical, 0 is unrelated.

    Intermezzo

    Gatecrashers

    Is that harmony real, or would any crowd do?

    Keep every guest exactly as sociable, but rewire who talks to whom at random. Then let the maître d’ seat that party.

    Random parties score above zero too. Report modularity against a null, never alone.

    Main course

    Turn up the volume

    Some pairs cannot stop talking.

    Hegel’s and Marx’s articles link to each other ten times. Count that as volume, the link’s weight. A guest’s total volume is their strength.

    Most conversations

      Most volume

        Weights can change the answer. Say which one you used.

        Cheese

        Only the conversations that matter

        A crowded room hides its structure. Keep only the conversations that matter.

        A conversation matters to a guest if it carries more of their volume than chance would give it. The disparity filter keeps a link when (1 − p)k−1 < α for either guest, where p is the link’s share of that guest’s volume and k is their degree.

        The loudest links are not the important ones. Quiet guests keep their one important conversation.

        Dessert

        Circles of friends

        Tables force every guest to choose. Circles do not.

        A k-clique is k guests who all talk to one another. Two k-cliques that share k − 1 guests join the same circle. A guest can stand in several circles, or in none.

          Overlapping communities: a guest may belong to many groups. Bigger k, tighter circles, more guests left out.

          Digestif

          The full hall

          Our party was 24 guests. The hall holds 1,374.

          Run the same maître d’ on every philosopher in the network, and these guests land in five schools. The medieval guests split into a Christian table and an Islamic and Jewish one. There were not enough of them at our dinner to show it.

          Communities depend on who is in the room.

          From the kitchen

          The party. 24 philosophers chosen by hand for recognisability, and every link among them in the Week 4 snapshot. A link means one Wikipedia article links to the other; volume is how many times, summed over both directions. A link is not friendship, agreement, or influence.

          The seating. Louvain, modularity and the shuffle are the from-scratch versions in prepare_data.py, run on this party by prepare_symposium.py with seeds 1–50. Shuffles are degree-preserving double-edge swaps. The replay is the recorded move order of each seed.

          Live on this page. Your plan’s harmony, NMI, the disparity filter and the loudest-links comparison are computed in your browser. Tests check that the harmony here matches the Python value on every evening.

          The hall. Colours in the digestif are the reference run of the full network (the run with the highest mean NMI to the other 49).

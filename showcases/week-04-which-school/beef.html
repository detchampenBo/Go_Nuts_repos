The Week 4 idea

A community is a pattern of connections.
Agreement is a different question.

The disputes come from philosophical texts. The groups come from the network. Putting the two together lets you test an interpretation, rather than treating a group’s name as a discovery.

Check the network.

1,374 philosophers; 9,139 undirected links. An edge means one Wikipedia article links to the other. It does not identify friendship, agreement, or historical influence.

How the results and drawing were made

The same unweighted Louvain implementation runs on the full network with seeds 1–50. Pair membership is checked in the original partitions, before any display alignment or merging. These are repeated algorithm results, not probabilities that a philosophical claim is true.

The drawing is a two-person ego view, not a backbone: at most 12 shared neighbours and 6 exclusive neighbours per side, ranked by full-network degree. Only links incident to the two featured philosophers are drawn. All counts and the complete neighbour list use the full graph.

Positions are arranged by connection to the pair, not by philosophical distance. Node radius is 2 + 0.8√degree. Colour uses the reference partition (the run with highest mean NMI to the other 49); tiny reference groups are combined in grey. The drawing stays fixed when inspecting runs.
